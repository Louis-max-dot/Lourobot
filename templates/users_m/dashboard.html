Who we are

Every enterprise is, at its core, an attempt to solve a coordination problem. How do you get thousands of people, across dozens of functions, working toward the same outcome? The answer, built up over decades, was process. Rules, hierarchies, handoff protocols, systems of record. Structures designed to compensate for what individual humans can't do: hold everything in memory, be in two places at once, never drop a thread. When agents became capable enough to handle genuine complexity, most deployments placed them into an enterprise context that was never designed for them: fragmented systems, no shared memory, no thread connecting this conversation to the last one. The intelligence was real, but the infrastructure underneath it wasn't built to sustain it. More AI producing more output doesn't challenge the fundamental assumption of enterprise architecture. It just accelerates the same loss. HappyRobot decided to build something different: not a better tool for the enterprise as it exists, but the foundation for a fundamentally different one.

"The machines that matter aren't the ones that analyze. They're the ones that act." by HappyRobot

Our Values

At LouRobot, we don't chase ideas, we ship systems that work, in production, every single day. We take ownership end-to-end, move with urgency, and hold a high bar for what "done" means. We've come together from across the world, not just for the ambition of the problems we solve, but for the standard we hold ourselves to while solving them.

Extreme ownership

We take responsibility for our work, our outcomes, and the company. Pick a problem, take it from idea to live, fully on you.

CraftmanShip

We put care into every customer interaction, every workflow, every line of code. The work is judged by what's running.

We are "majos"

A Spanish word, helpful, warm, low-ego. We have fun together and disagree without making it personal.

Talent density and meritocracy

We hire people who raise the average. Ability beats seniority. The best idea wins, regardless of who said it.

First-principle thinking

Strip the problem to its basic facts, ignore dogma, and design from scratch. No cargo culting.

Urgency with focus

Move fast, in the right direction. Maximum impact in the shortest time. Velocity compounds; bureaucracy never does.
